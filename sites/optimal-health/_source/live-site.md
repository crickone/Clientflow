It also helps your body clear out exercise waste products , and over a block of sessions can encourage new tiny blood vessels and stronger tissue.

Explore the answers to common queries about HBOT, and if you don’t find what you’re looking for, our team is always ready to answer any questions.
What is Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy (HBOT)?
Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy is a calm, guided session in a pressurised chamber where you breathe higher-concentration oxygen through a mask. The gentle pressure helps your body use more oxygen than usual, which may support steadier energy, a clearer head, better sleep, and day-to-day recovery. At Optimal Health & Recovery we offer mild HBOT for general wellness only; we don’t diagnose or treat medical conditions.
How long is a session and how often should I come?
A typical session here lasts 45–60 minutes. Most people begin with one to two sessions per week for three to four weeks, then adjust based on how they feel and what their week looks like.
What does it feel like inside the chamber?
Most people describe it as quiet and relaxing. At the start you’ll notice gentle pressure in your ears, similar to an aircraft landing. We’ll show you an easy equalising technique and stay in touch throughout so you’re comfortable.
Will I notice anything after the first session?
It varies from person to person. Some people feel clearer and more settled after the first visit; others notice sleep or energy changes after a few sessions. We keep it simple with short check-ins on energy, sleep, and general ease so you can decide if the rhythm is working.
Is HBOT a medical treatment?
No. Hospitals and accredited clinics use higher-pressure HBOT for specific medical indications under a doctor’s care. Our service is mild HBOT for general wellness goals such as energy, clarity, sleep support, and feeling more ready between training or busy days.
Who is HBOT suitable for?
Busy professionals, parents, over-40s building strength, and desk workers who want a calm recovery hour to support energy, focus, sleep routine, and everyday ease. If you have specific medical concerns, speak with your GP first.
Who should check with a GP before booking?
If you’re pregnant or trying to conceive, have ear or sinus problems, difficulty equalising pressure, heart or respiratory conditions, implanted medical devices, recent surgery, or significant claustrophobia, please consult your GP before using HBOT.
Is HBOT safe and are there side effects?
When run correctly, HBOT is generally considered safe. Possible effects include temporary ear or sinus pressure, mild ear discomfort if equalising is difficult, and occasionally short-lived vision shifts. We supervise sessions, brief you before you begin, and stop or adjust if anything feels off.
What should I wear and bring?
Come in comfortable everyday clothing and arrive hydrated. For safety in an oxygen-rich environment, avoid oily lotions, hairsprays, and perfumes just before your session. We’ll guide you on what can and cannot go into the chamber.
Can I read or use my phone?
We prefer a distraction-free hour so you can relax fully. If we allow a book or audio, we’ll confirm what’s safe to bring inside. Many people simply rest or nap.
What if I feel claustrophobic?
We can arrange a familiarisation sit-in with no pressure so you can see the space and practice equalising. You can communicate with us at any time during your session, and we can pause or depressurise if needed.
Can I come if I have a cold or sinus congestion?
It’s better to reschedule. Congestion can make equalising uncomfortable. Wait until you’re fully better, and check with your GP if you’re unsure.
Can I train on the same day as HBOT?
Yes. Many clients choose HBOT in the morning for clarity and train later, or train first and book Infrared in the evening for relaxation and sleep support. We’ll help you place sessions so you feel ready, not sluggish.
What should I do after a session?
Hydrate, move gently, and notice how your energy and sleep feel over the next 24 hours. There’s no downtime; most people return to work or normal activities straight away.
Can I combine HBOT with Infrared or PEMF?
Absolutely. A common routine is HBOT in the morning for focus and Infrared in the evening for wind-down and sleep. PEMF works well on desk-tight days when hips and back feel stiff. We’ll tailor the mix to your goals and schedule.
Are there people who should avoid HBOT altogether?
If you’ve had a recent pneumothorax, certain ear surgeries, or have devices or conditions that are incompatible with pressure changes, HBOT may not be suitable. Share your health history with us; we may ask for GP clearance before proceeding.
Can I do HBOT while pregnant?
Please get advice from your GP. We don’t proceed without appropriate clearance.
What’s the best way to start?
Book a quick 5-minute call. We’ll confirm suitability, suggest ideal timing — mornings for calm days or evenings for sleep — and plan your first week so you know exactly what to expect.

The Science of Light: A Quick Primer
At its core, all light therapy is a form of photobiomodulation—a term that simply means using light (photo) to change (modulate) a biological process (bio). This process works because our bodies, right down to the cellular level, are designed to react to light. Specific wavelengths of light can penetrate the skin and interact with our cells, particularly with tiny organelles called mitochondria.
Often referred to as the "powerhouses" of our cells, mitochondria are responsible for producing the energy currency known as ATP (adenosine triphosphate). When our cells are stressed by injury, inflammation, or physical exertion, their mitochondrial function can become impaired. This is where infrared therapy comes in. Certain wavelengths of red and near-infrared light act like a supercharger for your mitochondria, stimulating them to produce ATP more efficiently. This energy boost empowers your cells to repair damage, reduce inflammation, and function at their peak.
Demystifying the Technologies: What’s the Difference?

Targeted High-Intensity Laser Therapy
Think of a high-intensity laser as a spotlight. It delivers a very powerful, concentrated beam of light to a small, specific area. This modality is typically used in a clinical setting by a practitioner to treat acute injuries or deep, localized issues like a torn ligament or a specific joint problem. Common Red Light Therapy Panels
