The HIFEM chair, and a week spent sitting down

Sitting still isn't restful for the muscle that holds you up. What the chair does, and why you plan it like a gym session rather than a rest.

All articles · More on HIFEM

What a sitting week leaves behind

Eight hours at a desk, half an hour in the car each way, an evening on the sofa. Hips and lower back tighten, shoulders round forward, and the deep muscle that holds you upright spends most of the day doing almost nothing. It comes on so gradually that it ends up counting as normal.

The muscle that gets least out of a sitting week is also the hardest to find on purpose. Plenty of people can't feel the pelvic floor well enough to train it accurately, even when they're doing the exercises properly.

A contraction you didn't have to make

The chair generates a focused magnetic field. That field passes through clothing and skin, and where it reaches muscle it prompts the nerve to fire, so the muscle contracts. There's no more to the mechanism than that, and it's also why you keep your clothes on. A magnetic field goes straight through fabric, so nothing has to reach skin to work.

Two things separate it from a set of exercises: the rate, and the reach. You can't voluntarily contract a muscle thousands of times inside half an hour, and you can't hold a contraction at full effort without letting go to breathe. The field has neither limit.

Twenty to thirty minutes, in what you arrived in

You sit down, someone sets the intensity with you, and the session runs twenty to thirty minutes. You feel a firm, rhythmic pulsing in the muscle underneath you, and it stays where you want it the whole way through. Most people talk, read, or simply sit. There's nothing to change into and nothing to take off, and nothing about it is internal — including the pelvic-floor work.

It's muscle work, not a rest

People get this one wrong, so it's worth repeating. The bed and the chamber ask nothing of you and sit fine on a rest day. The chair doesn't. Plan it the way you'd plan a session in the gym — not stacked on top of a hard one, and not the day before something that matters until you know how you respond to it. Plenty of people put it on a light day, or after their main session.

Judge it over weeks

Everything above is an account of what the field is understood to do, not a forecast of what it'll do for you. It's “can” throughout rather than “will”. And because it's muscle work, it behaves like muscle work: one session won't shift much, and a few weeks of them might, the same as in a gym. So judge it over a run, and book a single session first if you'd rather find out whether you get on with sitting in it.
