The HIFEM chair, and a week spent sitting down

Sitting still is not restful for the muscle that holds you up. What the chair does, and why it is planned like a gym session rather than a rest.

All articles · More on HIFEM

What a sitting week leaves behind

Eight hours at a desk, half an hour in the car each way, an evening on the sofa. Hips and lower back tighten, shoulders round forward, and the deep muscle that holds you upright spends most of the day doing almost nothing. It arrives so gradually that it ends up counting as normal.

The muscle that gets least out of a sitting week is also the hardest to find on purpose. The pelvic floor is one a great many people cannot feel well enough to train accurately, even when they are doing the exercises properly.

A contraction you did not have to make

The chair generates a focused magnetic field. That field passes through clothing and skin, and where it reaches muscle it prompts the nerve to fire, so the muscle contracts. That is the whole of the mechanism, and it is also the reason you keep your clothes on: a magnetic field goes straight through fabric, so nothing has to reach skin to work.

What separates it from a set of exercises is the rate and the reach. You cannot voluntarily contract a muscle thousands of times inside half an hour, and you cannot hold a contraction at full effort without letting go to breathe. The field has neither limit.

Twenty to thirty minutes, in what you arrived in

You sit down, someone sets the intensity with you, and the session runs twenty to thirty minutes. What you feel is a firm, rhythmic pulsing in the muscle underneath you, adjusted with you rather than at you, so it stays where you want it the whole way through. Most people talk, read, or simply sit. There is nothing to change into and nothing to take off, and nothing about it is internal — including the pelvic-floor work.

It is muscle work, not a rest

This is the one people get wrong, so it is worth repeating. The bed and the chamber ask nothing of you and sit fine on a rest day. The chair does not. Plan it the way you would plan a session in the gym rather than assuming it is free — not stacked on top of a hard one, and not the day before something that matters until you know how you respond to it. Plenty of people put it on a light day, or after their main session.

What moves is repetition

Everything above is an account of what the field is understood to do, not a forecast of what it will do for you. It is “can” throughout rather than “will”, and we would rather say so out loud than leave the verbs to carry it. Because it is muscle work it behaves like muscle work: what moves is repetition over weeks, the same as it is in a gym. Judge it over a run of sessions rather than over one, and book a single session first if you would rather find out whether you get on with sitting in it.
