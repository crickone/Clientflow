Panels, lasers, and a bed you lie down in

Light therapy is not one thing. A hand-held laser, a panel on a stand and a full-length bed do different jobs, and the difference is mostly how much of you the light reaches.

All articles · More on infrared

One idea, three machines

All three work on the same principle. Particular wavelengths of light pass into tissue, cells absorb them, and the working account is that the part of the cell which makes its energy can make more of it when they do. What differs is not the idea. It is how the light is delivered, how far it carries, and how much of you is in front of it at once.

A laser is a spotlight

A high-intensity laser puts a powerful, concentrated beam into a small area. That is useful when there is one specific place to aim at, and it is generally used by a practitioner in a clinical setting for exactly that. The power lets the light carry deep; the coverage is a few square centimetres. It is a tool for a target rather than for a body.

A panel is a floodlight

A red-light panel uses an array of LEDs to cast light across a broader area — a face, a back, a limb. Coverage goes up and the power at any one point goes down. Panels suit surface-level work, skin most of all, and covering more than one part of you means either more sessions or standing there rearranging yourself between them.

The bed is neither

You lie down in it and the light reaches you head to toe at once, red and near-infrared together. Red does more at the surface; near-infrared carries furthest into tissue. Nothing has to be aimed, nothing has to be repositioned, and no part of you is waiting its turn. That is the whole argument for a bed over the other two, and it is an argument about coverage rather than about power.

Why twelve minutes

Because that is how long the bed runs. Full-length coverage is what makes a short session worth booking: there is no sequence of areas to work through, so the session is the same length whether you came in about your shoulders or about your legs. The whole visit sits inside half an hour, there is nothing to change into, and there is no downtime afterwards. It is the easiest thing in the building to fit around a working week, which is most of why people keep coming back to it.

Two things it is not

It is not a sunbed. The bed emits red and near-infrared light — 633, 660, 810, 850 and 940 nm — and no ultraviolet at all, so there is no tanning and no burning.

And it is not a medical treatment. We do not diagnose anything and we make no claim to resolve anything. If you have a diagnosed condition, follow your clinician’s advice and tell us about it before you book.
