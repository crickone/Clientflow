Panels, lasers, and a bed you lie down in

A hand-held laser, a panel and a full-length bed all use light, but they reach very different amounts of you.

All articles · More on infrared

One idea, three machines

All three work the same way: cells absorb particular wavelengths of light and can make more energy as a result. They differ in power, depth and how much of you the light reaches at once.

A laser is a spotlight

A high-intensity laser puts a powerful, narrow beam into a small area. It reaches deep but covers only a few square centimetres, so it's used on one specific spot, usually by a clinician.

A panel is a floodlight

A red-light panel uses LEDs to cover a wider area, like a face, back or limb, at lower power. It suits surface work such as skin, but covering more of you means more sessions or repositioning.

The bed covers all of you

You lie in it and the light reaches you head to toe at once. Red works mostly at the surface; near-infrared reaches deeper. Nothing needs aiming or repositioning.

The light only works where it meets skin, so you undress for the bed. Underwear is fine, and you're on your own in the room.

Why twelve minutes

Because the whole body is covered at once, there's nothing to work through area by area, so the session stays at twelve minutes. The whole visit takes under half an hour, with no downtime (allow a few minutes to undress).

Two things it isn't

It's not a sunbed: there's no ultraviolet, so no tanning and no burning.

And it isn't a medical treatment. We don't diagnose or treat anything. If you have a diagnosed condition, follow your doctor's advice and tell us before you book.
