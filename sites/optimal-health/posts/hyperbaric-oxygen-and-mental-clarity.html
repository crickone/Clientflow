Hyperbaric oxygen and mental clarity after a hard week

Forty-five to sixty minutes in a pressurised chamber, breathing air with more oxygen in it than the room has. What the hour is like, and what people report from it.

All articles · More on hyperbaric oxygen

When people book it

After a run of late finishes and poor sleep, when your head feels a step behind and everything takes longer than it should.

What the hour is like

You sit in a chamber and breathe air with more oxygen than the room has, through a soft mask, while the pressure rises slowly over the first few minutes. Your ears fill a little, like a plane landing, and clear when you swallow. Most people read, rest or doze.

Why the pressure matters

At normal pressure, almost all the oxygen you breathe is carried by red blood cells, which are nearly full already, so breathing harder doesn't add much. Under pressure, oxygen also dissolves into the plasma, so your blood carries more of it further into tissue. The body uses oxygen to repair itself.

What people report

Clients most often describe clarity and steadiness: a less cluttered head and an afternoon that doesn't slump. That's what people report, not a promise.

It builds over weeks

One session won't do much on its own. Over a run of sessions, the body can also grow new capillaries, the smallest blood vessels, and lay down stronger collagen. Most people give it a few weeks before deciding.

What it isn't

It isn't a medical treatment, and we don't diagnose anything. If something is wrong, see your doctor first. Tell us about any diagnosed condition before you book.
