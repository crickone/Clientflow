Hyperbaric oxygen and mental clarity after a hard week

Forty-five to sixty minutes in a pressurised chamber, breathing air with more oxygen in it than the room has. What the hour is like, and what people report from it.

The week this tends to get booked after

A run of late finishes. Sleep that went off somewhere around Tuesday. A head that feels a step behind the day, where the work still gets done but every part of it takes longer than it should. Nobody needs a name for that week to recognise it, and most people who book the chamber aren't looking for one.

What the hour is like

You sit in a clear, comfortable chamber and breathe air with a higher concentration of oxygen than the room has, while the pressure inside is raised slowly over the first few minutes. You breathe through a soft mask, and nothing else is attached to you. Most people notice the pressure the way they'd notice it on a plane coming in to land: a soft fullness in the ears that clears when they swallow. After that they stop noticing it for the rest of the hour. Most read, rest, or doze.

Why the pressure is the point

At room pressure, nearly all the oxygen you breathe rides on red blood cells, and those cells are close to full already, so breathing harder doesn't add much. Pressure changes that. It dissolves oxygen straight into the plasma, the fluid those cells travel in, so your blood carries more of it than it otherwise could and can bring it further into tissue. Oxygen is the fuel the body repairs itself with, and a session is simply an hour of having more of it to hand than usual.

What people report

The words that come back most often are clarity and steadiness, not energy. People describe a head that feels less cluttered, and an afternoon that doesn't fall off a cliff. That's what clients tell us afterwards, not an outcome anyone can promise you.

One hour is one hour

One hour on its own won't do much. A run of them might. Given more oxygen more often, the body can also respond by growing new capillaries, the smallest blood vessels and the ones that reach where the larger ones don't. It can lay down stronger collagen too, the protein that gives skin, tendon and connective tissue their structure. Both of those are a “can” over weeks rather than a “will” over an hour, and most people give the chamber a few weeks before deciding.

What it isn't

It's not a medical treatment, and we don't diagnose anything. If something is actually wrong, that's a conversation for your doctor before it's a conversation for us, and we'd tell you so on the phone. Tell us about any diagnosed condition before you book and we'll be straight with you about whether a session suits. The clarity and the steadier afternoons are what people say, and that's all we're claiming for them.
