Hyperbaric oxygen and mental clarity after a hard week

Forty-five to sixty minutes in a pressurised chamber, breathing air with more oxygen in it than the room has. What the hour is like, and what people report from it.

The week this tends to get booked after

A run of late finishes. Sleep that went off somewhere around Tuesday. A head that feels a step behind the day, where the work still gets done but every part of it takes longer than it should. Nobody needs a name for that week to recognise it, and most people who book the chamber are not looking for one.

What the hour is

You sit in a clear, comfortable chamber and breathe air with a higher concentration of oxygen than the room has, while the pressure inside is raised slowly over the first few minutes. You breathe through a soft, comfortable mask, and that is the only thing attached to you. Most people notice the pressure the way they would on a plane coming in to land — a soft fullness in the ears that clears when they swallow — and then stop noticing it for the rest of the hour. Most rest, listen to something, or doze.

Why the pressure is the point

At room pressure almost all the oxygen you breathe is carried by red blood cells, and those cells are close to full already, so breathing harder does not add much. Pressure changes that. It dissolves oxygen directly into the plasma, the fluid those cells travel in, so your blood carries more of it than it otherwise could and can bring it further into tissue. Oxygen is the fuel the body repairs itself with, and a session is simply an hour of having more of it to hand than usual.

What people report

The words that come back most often are clarity and steadiness rather than energy: a head that feels less cluttered, and an afternoon that does not fall off a cliff. It is worth being careful about how that gets written down. It is what clients tell us afterwards, not an outcome anyone can promise you, and the honest version of that sentence keeps the caveat attached rather than tucking it away.

One hour is one hour

What builds is the series. Given more oxygen more often, the body can also respond by growing new capillaries — the smallest blood vessels, the ones that reach where the larger ones do not — and by laying down stronger collagen, the protein that gives skin, tendon and connective tissue their structure. Both of those are a “can” over weeks rather than a “will” over an hour, and most people judge the chamber after a few weeks of sessions rather than after one.

What it is not

It is not a medical treatment, and we do not diagnose anything. If something is actually wrong, that is a conversation for your doctor before it is a conversation for us, and we would tell you so on the phone. Tell us about any diagnosed condition before you book and we will be straight with you about whether a session suits. The rest of it — the clarity, the steadier afternoons — is what people say, offered as what people say.
