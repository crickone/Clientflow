Infrared and everyday aches: what the light actually does

Twelve minutes under red and near-infrared light. What the bed emits, what the light is understood to do once it's in tissue, and what people say afterwards.

All articles · More on infrared

The thing people actually come in about

Shoulders that have set after a week at a desk. Hips that complain after a long drive. A back that is louder some weeks than others, and a stiffness first thing in the morning that wasn't there a few years ago. None of that has to have a name before it's worth doing something about, and most people who book the bed couldn't give you one if you asked.

What the bed emits

Red and near-infrared light at five wavelengths: 633, 660, 810, 850 and 940 nm, and no ultraviolet at all. Those numbers are a specification of the machine, the way a kettle has a wattage. Red is the part you can see. Near-infrared is the part you can't, and it carries furthest into tissue.

It's not a sunbed. A sunbed works by emitting ultraviolet light, and this bed emits none. There's no tanning and no burning, and nothing about a session is meant to change the colour of your skin.

What the light is understood to do

The mechanism, as far as anyone understands it, runs like this. Light at those wavelengths passes into skin and the connective tissue under it much as sunlight passes through a window, and cells absorb it the way a solar panel absorbs daylight. Inside the cell, the mitochondria do the absorbing — they're the part that makes the cell’s energy — and the working account is that they can make more usable energy when they do.

With more of that energy to hand, a cell can do more of its ordinary work. Small blood vessels can open a little wider, so more oxygen and nutrients reach the area being warmed. The cells that make collagen can lay down more of it. The signalling that keeps tissue irritated can settle.

Every one of those is a “can” rather than a “will”. They're what the process is understood to encourage over a run of sessions, not what one session delivers.

What it feels like

Deep, even warmth that should feel soothing rather than hot. You lie back fully clothed and the bed warms up gradually. There's little sweating to speak of, no effort, and nothing to recover from afterwards. If it ever feels too warm, say so — we can turn it down or stop.

What people say afterwards

Looser through the hips, back and shoulders, and warm for a while after. People often say the stiffness that follows sitting or training is easier the next morning, and a good number say they slept better the night of a session. That's what clients report, not what we can promise.

What it isn't

It's not a medical treatment. We run wellness-focused infrared warmth for relaxation, comfort and rest. We don't diagnose anything and we make no claim to resolve anything. If you have a diagnosed condition, follow your clinician’s advice, and tell us about it before you book so we can say honestly whether this suits you.
