Stress, sleep, and the twelve minutes after work

An evening infrared session gives you half an hour to wind down, and booking it means you'll actually take it.

All articles · More on infrared

A week that never quite switches off

Most people who book an evening session aren't unwell. They've had a run of busy weeks: late finishes, a phone that won't stop, sleep that's gone off.

What happens in the twelve minutes

Twelve minutes on the infrared bed, undressed. The light has to land on skin to get in, so the less you have on the more of you it reaches; underwear is fine, and you're on your own in the room. The bed gives off red and near-infrared light, not hot air, so you barely sweat, and there's nothing to recover from afterwards. The whole visit sits inside half an hour.

People book it for the evening because it's easier to wind down afterwards. Plenty fall asleep on it.

The quiet room helps too

Part of what an evening session gives you is a quiet room for a set time, with your phone put away.

And a booked session is easier to keep than a good intention.

What people say about the night after

Clients most often say the evening wound down more easily and they slept better that night. That's what they report, not a promise.

For a different kind of tired

If a week has left you sluggish rather than wound up, people tend to book the hyperbaric chamber instead: forty-five to sixty minutes breathing extra oxygen under pressure. People describe a clearer head and steadier energy afterwards.

What this isn't

None of this is a medical treatment, and it's no substitute for sleep, food or your doctor's advice. If you have a diagnosed condition, follow their advice and tell us before you book.
