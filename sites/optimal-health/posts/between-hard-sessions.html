Between hard sessions: where the chamber fits in a training week

Recovery belongs in your training plan. What an hour in the chamber involves, and where it fits in the week.

All articles · More for athletes

Put recovery in the diary

Most people who train seriously plan their sessions and leave recovery to whatever time is left, which in a busy week is none. Booking an hour in the diary fixes most of that.

What the hour involves

You sit in a chamber and breathe air with more oxygen than the room has, through a soft mask, while the pressure rises slowly over the first few minutes. Your ears fill a little, like a plane landing, and clear when you swallow. A session runs forty-five to sixty minutes.

Why the pressure changes anything

At normal pressure, almost all the oxygen you breathe is carried by red blood cells, which are nearly full already, so breathing harder doesn't add much. Under pressure, oxygen also dissolves into the plasma, so your blood carries more of it further into tissue. The body uses oxygen to repair itself.

Where it goes in the week

It adds no training load, so it fits after a hard session, between two, or on a rest day. People who train most mornings tend to book it the evening of their hardest session.

It builds over weeks

One session won't do much on its own. Over a run of sessions, the body can also respond by growing new capillaries, the smallest blood vessels. Most people judge it over a few weeks.
