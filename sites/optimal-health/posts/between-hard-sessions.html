Between hard sessions: where the chamber fits in a training week

How you recover is part of the training week rather than an afterthought to it. Where an hour in the chamber sits in that week, and what two competitors who use it say.

All articles · More for athletes

Recovery is a slot in the week, not the gap between weeks

Most people who train seriously plan their sessions and leave everything else to chance. The week gets built around the hard days, and recovery is whatever is left once work, family and sleep have taken their share, which in a busy week is nothing. Putting an hour in the diary is most of the idea here, and it is a scheduling decision before it is anything else.

What the hour actually is

You sit in a clear, comfortable chamber and breathe air with a higher concentration of oxygen than the room has, while the pressure inside is raised slowly over the first few minutes. You breathe through a soft mask, the only thing attached to you. Most people notice the pressure the way they would on a plane coming in to land. The ears fill a little and clear when you swallow, and then you stop noticing it. A session runs forty-five to sixty minutes.

Why the pressure changes anything

At room pressure almost all the oxygen you breathe is carried by red blood cells, and those cells are close to full already, so breathing harder does not add much. Pressure dissolves oxygen directly into the plasma, the fluid those cells travel in, so your blood carries more of it than it otherwise could and can bring it further into tissue. Oxygen is the fuel the body repairs itself with. An hour in the chamber is an hour of having more of it to hand than usual.

Where it goes in the week

It adds no training load of its own, so it sits fine after a hard session, on the day between two, or on a rest day. People who train five or six mornings a week tend to put it on the evening of the hardest one. Nothing about it asks anything of you. A recovery slot that costs you effort is just another session.

The one that is not free

The chair is the exception, and it is worth being straight about because it is the thing people get wrong. HIFEM is muscle work, not a rest-day therapy. Plan it the way you would plan a gym session, and if you have never sat in it, do not put the first one the day before something that matters. The bed and the chamber carry no such caveat.

One session is one session

What builds is the series. Given more oxygen more often, the body can also respond by growing new capillaries — the smallest blood vessels, the ones that reach where the larger ones do not — and that is a “can” over weeks rather than a “will” over an hour. Most people judge it over a few weeks of sessions.

We are not going to tell you it will take time off anything. What it is is an hour a week spent recovering on purpose, instead of hoping the week leaves room for it.
