What collagen is.

Collagen is the protein that keeps skin firm and elastic and gives tendons and ligaments their strength.

Your body makes and breaks down collagen all your life. Age, sun, training load, hormones and injury tip the balance towards breaking it down. When that happens, skin loses firmness, and tendons and joints feel it more under load.

A run of sessions can shift that balance a little back towards making collagen, over months.

How the bed works.

The bed gives off red and near-infrared light at five wavelengths: 633, 660, 810, 850 and 940 nm. You can see the red light. Near-infrared light is invisible and reaches deepest into tissue.

This is called photobiomodulation. The light passes into your skin and the tissue beneath it. There, the mitochondria (the part of the cell that makes energy) absorb it and can then make more energy.

Fibroblasts, the cells that make collagen, can use that energy to lay down more of it. The enzymes that break collagen down are thought to slow at the same time, and circulation can improve, bringing in the materials to build with.

You undress for the bed, because clothing blocks the light. The less you wear, the more of your skin it reaches; underwear is fine. You're on your own in the room.

What to expect over twelve weeks.

This is what the research suggests is happening at each stage. Use it as a rough guide.

  1. Weeks 1–2

    Circulation first.

    In the first two weeks, the light is thought to work on cell energy and circulation. Blood flow can increase and inflammation can settle, so skin may look better hydrated and tissue may feel calmer. No collagen has been rebuilt yet.

  2. Weeks 4–6

    Building collagen.

    Collagen-making cells can start laying down new collagen, along with the molecules that hold water in the skin. Skin can feel springier and better hydrated, and collagen in tendons and ligaments starts to organise. Eating enough protein and vitamin C helps, because the body builds collagen from them.

  3. Weeks 8–12+

    Reorganising.

    Later, the collagen fibres are thought to reorganise and strengthen, while the enzymes that break collagen down slow. Connective tissue may handle load more comfortably. After twelve weeks, the aim is to keep it up.
