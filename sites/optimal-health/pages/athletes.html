Four therapies, and what each is for.

The short version of each. Every therapy has its own page with the details.

None of these replace training, sleep or food.

Hyperbaric oxygen

45–60 minutes · you sit still

The one athletes here book most. You breathe extra oxygen in a pressurised chamber, so your blood carries more of it than usual. Most people book it the day after a match, race or long run, and through heavy weeks.

See hyperbaric oxygen

Infrared

12 minutes · you lie down

Twelve minutes under red and near-infrared light, undressed so it reaches your skin. The easiest to fit around training: no load, nothing to recover from, fine on a rest day or between two sessions. People book it for next-day stiffness and to wind down in the evening.

See infrared

The HIFEM chair

20–30 minutes · muscle work

You sit fully clothed while a focused magnetic field contracts the muscle underneath. It's muscle work, so plan it like a gym session: not on top of a hard one, and not the day before something important until you know how you respond.

See HIFEM

Massage

30–60 minutes · hands-on

Therapeutic massage, reflexology and lymphatic drainage from clinical therapists. Book it when something has been tight for a while, and tell us whether you want it working or relaxing.

Where it goes in the week.

The five times athletes most often book. If your week looks different, ring us.

  1. Post-session

    The same evening, or the day after.

    The most common booking: infrared in the evening after a heavy lift or long run, or the chamber the day after a match or race. Neither adds training load. People say the first thing they notice is less next-day stiffness and lighter legs in the next session.

  2. Pre-event

    Not the week to try something new.

    If you want the chamber or the bed before a race or match, use them in the weeks before too, so you know how you respond. The same goes for the chair: it's muscle work and belongs in your taper like any other session.

  3. Niggles

    Alongside your physio's plan.

    We don't assess injuries or build rehab plans. If you're working with a physio or doctor, follow their plan and use us around it, with their agreement. Tell us what you've been told to avoid.

  4. Heavy blocks

    Little and often, through the loaded weeks.

    Through a heavy block or run of fixtures, short regular sessions work better than one long visit after things have gone wrong. Two or three infrared sessions a week is common; the chamber once or twice.

  5. Travel days

    After the bus, and between the rounds.

    Long journeys leave you stiff, and multi-day events leave little time between rounds. Infrared fits the gaps best: twelve minutes and nothing to recover from (allow a few minutes to undress). The chamber needs a longer slot, so it suits the evening before or the morning after.

What athletes ask us first.

Will this make me faster?

No. Training makes you faster; sleep and food decide how much of it you keep. Recovery supports the time in between. Judge it by whether your next training block goes better than the last.

Does a session take anything out of me?

Infrared, hyperbaric oxygen and massage don't. There's nothing to recover from, so any of them suits a rest day.

The chair is the exception: plan it like a gym session, and don't try it for the first time the day before something important.

When in the week should I book?

If you're coming once, book it after your hardest session. Regulars tend to use the chamber in the morning and the bed in the evening, and fit the chair and massage around them.

Tell us your week and we'll recommend what's worth it.

I'm coming back from an injury. Can I use this?

Check with your physio or doctor first. We don't assess injuries or build rehab plans, but we can work within one.

I get tested. Is any of this a problem?

Nothing here involves a substance: it's oxygen under pressure, light, a magnetic field and massage. Your governing body sets the rules, so if you're in a testing pool, check with your federation or anti-doping body before you book. We'll give them any equipment details they need.

Do I have to buy a block?

You don't have to, but we recommend it. Recovery works over a run of sessions, and a block lowers the price of each one.
