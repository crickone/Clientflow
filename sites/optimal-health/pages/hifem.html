A chair, and you keep your clothes on.

HIFEM stands for High-Intensity Focused Electromagnetic fields. The machine is a chair: you sit down in whatever you arrived in, someone sets the intensity with you, and the session runs twenty to thirty minutes.

You feel a firm, rhythmic pulsing in the muscle underneath you. We set the intensity with you and change it the moment you say, so it stays where you want it. Most people talk, read, or just sit.

Plenty of people book this one for pelvic floor support. Nothing about it is internal, and you stay seated and dressed for that too. Ring and ask before you come in if that's easier — we'd far rather you did.

In their words

A contraction you did not have to make.

The chair generates a focused magnetic field. It passes through clothing and skin, and where it reaches muscle it prompts the nerve to fire, so the muscle contracts. That's all of it, and that's why you stay dressed.

It goes faster and reaches further than exercises can. You can't contract a muscle thousands of times in half an hour on your own, and you can't hold full effort without letting go to breathe. The field has neither limit, and it can reach muscle that's hard to find on purpose. The pelvic floor is the clearest case: plenty of people can't feel it well enough to train it accurately even when they're doing the exercises properly.

All of that is what the field is understood to do, not a forecast of what it will do for you. We say “can” and not “will” all the way through, and we mean it. And this is muscle work, so it behaves like muscle work: it builds up with repetition over weeks, the same as in a gym. Judge it over a run, and book one session first if you'd rather find out whether you get on with sitting in it.

What people ask before they book.

Do I have to undress?

No. You stay fully clothed and seated for the whole session, pelvic-floor sessions included. Comfortable everyday clothes are all you need, and there's nothing to change into.

What does it feel like?

A firm, rhythmic pulsing in the muscle under you, running in bursts with short pauses between them. It should feel strong rather than sharp, and people don't describe it as warm. You can adjust the intensity the whole way through and we start low, so tell us at any point and we'll bring it down.

How long is a session, and how often should I come?

Twenty to thirty minutes in the chair. Most people start with two or three sessions a week for a few weeks, then adjust around how they feel and what the week looks like.

How many before I notice anything?

It varies, and we'd rather say so than give you a number we can't stand over. Some people find hips and lower back looser after the first session. Pelvic-floor goals generally take a few weeks of regular sessions, alongside the daily habits, before there's much to judge.

Is this the same as doing pelvic-floor exercises?

No, and it isn't a replacement for them. Pelvic-floor exercises are contractions you make yourself, and part of the point is that you learn to find the muscle. The chair prompts the contraction for you, which helps most when the muscle is hard to locate. We put it alongside your daily habits and any guidance from a women's health physio, not instead of either.

Can I use it if I have pelvic pain, or a prolapse?

That one needs clinical guidance first. Speak with your GP or a pelvic-health physio before booking, so somebody qualified decides whether this is appropriate for you rather than us guessing.

Is this a medical treatment?

No. Electromagnetic muscle stimulation is used under clinician supervision for specific pelvic-health and musculoskeletal indications, with screening and protocols set for the individual. That's a different thing from what we run. At Optimal Health and Recovery this is a wellness session, for engagement, mobility and everyday comfort. We don't diagnose medical conditions and we don't claim to resolve them.

Who should not use it?

Anyone with a pacemaker, a neurostimulator or another implanted electronic device should not use it, and that one isn't open to discussion. If you're pregnant, have metal implants, or have had surgery recently, talk to your GP before you book. And if a clinician has already advised you against electromagnetic exposure, this isn't the right therapy for you.

Tell us before you book rather than on the day. If there's any doubt at all we'll wait for your GP's answer. A week's delay is a much smaller thing than getting this one wrong.

What if I have a metal implant or an old injury?

Tell us about every implant and every surgery, however long ago. Some metalwork is fine and some isn't, and it's not a judgement we make on the spot. We defer to your GP or your surgeon, and we'll suggest something else if the answer is no.

Can I book while pregnant, or while trying to conceive?

Speak with your GP first. We don't go ahead during pregnancy without appropriate clearance, and if it's not advised we'll point you at one of the other therapies instead.

How soon after having a baby can I use it?

Wait until after your six-week postnatal check, and only once you've been cleared for exercise there. Tell us about any tearing or a caesarean section when you book, and we'll make sure it's appropriate before your first session.

Is it safe, and are there side effects?

For anyone who clears the pacemaker, implant and pregnancy questions above, it's generally well tolerated. Some people notice temporary tingling, or mild short-lived fatigue in the muscles that were worked, much like light activation work in a gym. We supervise every session, the intensity is adjustable throughout, and we stop if anything feels off.

Will I be sore afterwards?

Most people get up feeling loose. A few notice the same mild heaviness you'd get from a light set, and it passes inside a day. Water and gentle movement usually settle it.

Can I train on the same day?

Yes, but put it somewhere deliberate. This is muscle work rather than a rest-day therapy, so plan it the way you'd plan a session in the gym rather than assuming it's free. Plenty of people put it on a light day or after their main session.

Can I combine it with the other therapies?

Yes, and most regulars do. A common week is hyperbaric oxygen in the morning for a clear head and infrared in the evening for wind-down and sleep, with the chair or a massage placed where they suit you. Tell us what your week looks like and we'll space them sensibly.
