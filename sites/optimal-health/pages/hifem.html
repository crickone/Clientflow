What people ask before they book.

Do I have to undress?

No. You stay fully clothed for the whole session, pelvic floor sessions included.

What does it feel like?

A firm, rhythmic pulsing in the muscle under you, in bursts with short pauses. It should feel strong rather than sharp. We start low and turn it down whenever you ask.

How many before I notice anything?

It varies. Some people find their hips and lower back looser after the first session. Pelvic floor goals usually take a few weeks of regular sessions.

Is this the same as doing pelvic-floor exercises?

No, and it doesn't replace them. Exercises are contractions you make yourself; the chair makes the muscle contract for you, which helps most when it's hard to find. Use it alongside your exercises and any advice from a women's health physio.

Can I use it if I have pelvic pain, or a prolapse?

Speak with your GP or a pelvic health physio before booking.

Is this a medical treatment?

No. It's a wellness session for muscle engagement, mobility and everyday comfort. We don't diagnose or treat medical conditions.

Who should not use it?

Anyone with a pacemaker, neurostimulator or other implanted electronic device must not use it. If you're pregnant, have metal implants or have had recent surgery, talk to your GP before booking. If a clinician has advised you against electromagnetic exposure, this isn't for you.

Tell us before you book. If there's any doubt, we'll wait for your GP's answer.

What if I have a metal implant or an old injury?

Tell us about every implant and surgery, however long ago. Some metalwork is fine and some isn't; we go by your GP's or surgeon's advice.

Can I book while pregnant, or while trying to conceive?

Speak with your GP first. We don't go ahead during pregnancy without appropriate clearance, and if it's not advised we'll point you at one of the other therapies instead.

How soon after having a baby can I use it?

Wait until after your six-week postnatal check, and only once you've been cleared for exercise there. Tell us about any tearing or a caesarean section when you book, and we'll make sure it's appropriate before your first session.

Is it safe, and are there side effects?

It's generally well tolerated by anyone who clears the questions above. Some people notice brief tingling or mild tiredness in the worked muscles, like after a light gym session. We supervise every session and stop if anything feels off.

Will I be sore afterwards?

Most people get up feeling loose. A few notice the same mild heaviness you'd get from a light set, and it passes inside a day. Water and gentle movement usually settle it.

Can I train on the same day?

Yes, but plan it. It's muscle work, so treat it like a gym session. Many people put it on a light day or after their main session.

Can I combine it with the other therapies?

Yes, and most regulars do. A common week: hyperbaric oxygen in the morning, infrared in the evening, and the chair or a massage where they fit. Tell us your week and we'll space them out.
