What it feels like.

You sit on the chair in the clothes you came in. You'll feel a firm, rhythmic pulsing in the muscles you're sitting on. It should feel strong, but not sharp. It runs in bursts with short pauses in between.

You can talk, read or just sit while your muscles work.

Many people book it for pelvic floor support. Nothing is internal, and you stay seated and dressed. If you have any questions, ring us before you come in.

Common questions.

Do I have to undress?

No. You stay fully clothed for the whole session, pelvic floor sessions included.

What does it feel like?

You'll feel a firm, rhythmic pulsing in the muscles you're sitting on. It comes in bursts with short pauses. It should feel strong, but not sharp. We start low and turn it down whenever you ask.

How many sessions before I notice a difference?

It varies. Some people find their hips and lower back feel looser after the first session. For the pelvic floor, it usually takes a few weeks of regular sessions.

Is this the same as doing pelvic-floor exercises?

No, and it doesn't replace them. With exercises, you contract the muscle yourself. HIFEM makes the muscle contract for you, which helps most if you find the muscle hard to feel. Keep doing your exercises and follow any advice from a women's health physio.

Can I use it if I have pelvic pain, or a prolapse?

Speak with your GP or a pelvic health physio before booking.

Is this a medical treatment?

No. It's a wellness session to work your muscles and help with movement and everyday comfort. We don't diagnose or treat medical conditions.

Who should not use it?

Anyone with a pacemaker, neurostimulator or other implanted electronic device must not use it. If you're pregnant, have metal implants or have had recent surgery, talk to your GP before booking. If a doctor or other clinician has told you to avoid electromagnetic exposure, you should not use it.

Tell us about any of these before you book. If there's any doubt, we'll wait until your GP says it's OK.

What if I have a metal implant or an old injury?

Tell us about every implant and surgery, however long ago. Some metal implants are fine and some aren't. We follow your GP's or surgeon's advice.

Can I book while pregnant, or while trying to conceive?

Speak with your GP first. We won't go ahead during pregnancy unless your GP has cleared it. If it's not advised, we'll suggest one of our other therapies instead.

How soon after having a baby can I use it?

Wait until after your six-week postnatal check, and only once you've been cleared for exercise at that check. When you book, tell us if you had any tearing or a caesarean section. We'll make sure it's suitable for you before your first session.

Is it safe, and are there side effects?

It is generally well tolerated by anyone who passes the checks above. Some people notice brief tingling or mild tiredness in the muscles afterwards, like after a light gym session. A member of our team supervises every session, and we stop if anything doesn't feel right.

Will I be sore afterwards?

Most people feel loose afterwards. A few people notice a mild heaviness in the muscles, like after a light workout. It goes within a day. Drinking water and moving gently usually helps.

Can I train on the same day?

Yes. Many people book it on a light training day or after their main workout.

Can I combine it with the other therapies?

Yes, and most of our regular clients do. For example, some people book hyperbaric oxygen in the morning, infrared in the evening, and fit in HIFEM or a massage around those. Tell us what your week looks like and we'll help you space them out.
