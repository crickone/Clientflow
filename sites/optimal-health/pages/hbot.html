You keep your clothes on, sit down in the chamber and breathe oxygen through a mask. The pressure comes up over the first few minutes. You feel it in your ears the way you do when a plane comes in to land, it clears when you swallow, and then you stop noticing it.

After that it is quiet. Most people read, rest or doze, and there is nothing to learn and nothing to do. People tend to come out saying they feel clear and settled.

Oxygen under gentle pressure.

  • Session length

    Including the few minutes it takes to come up to pressure and back down.

  • How often

    One or two a week

    For three or four weeks, then judge it. One session on its own will not do much.

  • Mostly booked for

    A clearer head

    Then recovery between training days, and sleep that holds through the night.

At room pressure nearly all the oxygen you breathe is carried by red blood cells, and those are close to full already. Breathing harder adds very little. Pressure changes that. It pushes oxygen straight into the plasma, the fluid the cells travel in, so the blood carries more of it and can take it further into tissue. And the body repairs itself with oxygen.

It builds over a run of sessions rather than in one. Over a block the body can also respond by growing new capillaries, the small vessels that reach where larger ones do not, and by laying down more collagen.

What people ask before their first session.

Is this a medical treatment?

No. Hospitals and accredited clinics use higher-pressure hyperbaric oxygen for specific medical indications, under a doctor's care. We offer mild hyperbaric oxygen for general wellness goals: energy, clarity, sleep, and feeling more ready between training or busy days. We don't diagnose or treat medical conditions.

Who is it suitable for?

Busy professionals, parents, people over forty building strength, and desk workers who want a calm hour that supports energy, focus, sleep routine and everyday ease. If you have specific medical concerns, speak with your GP first.

What if I feel claustrophobic?

We can arrange a familiarisation sit-in with no pressure at all, so you can see the space and practise equalising before you commit to a session. You can talk to us at any point once you're inside, and we can pause or depressurise if needed.

What should I wear, and what can I bring in?

Comfortable everyday clothes, and arrive hydrated. The air in the chamber is oxygen-rich, so leave off oily lotions, hairsprays and perfumes just before your session.

A book is fine, and plenty of people bring one. Electronics are not: no phone, no headphones, no tablet, no e-reader. That is a safety rule, not a preference about how you spend the hour. Leave anything electronic with us at the desk and we will keep it safe.

Plenty of people bring nothing at all and just rest or nap, and that is what we would suggest for a first session.

Will I notice anything after the first session?

It varies from person to person. Some people feel clearer and more settled after the first visit; others notice sleep or energy change after a few. We keep short check-ins on energy, sleep and general ease, so you can decide for yourself whether the rhythm is working.

What should I do afterwards?

Hydrate, move gently, and notice how your energy and sleep feel over the next twenty-four hours. There's no downtime. Most people go straight back to work or on with the day.

Is it safe, and are there side effects?

Run correctly, mild hyperbaric oxygen is generally considered safe. Possible effects include temporary ear or sinus pressure, mild ear discomfort if equalising is difficult, and occasionally short-lived shifts in vision. We supervise every session, brief you before you begin, and stop or adjust if anything feels off.

Who should check with a GP before booking?

If you're pregnant or trying to conceive, have ear or sinus problems, difficulty equalising pressure, heart or respiratory conditions, implanted medical devices, recent surgery, or significant claustrophobia, please talk to your GP before using hyperbaric oxygen.

Are there people who should avoid it altogether?

If you've had a recent pneumothorax, certain ear surgeries, or have devices or conditions that aren't compatible with pressure changes, hyperbaric oxygen may not be suitable for you. Tell us your health history, and we may ask for GP clearance before going ahead.

Can I come while pregnant?

Please get advice from your GP. We don't proceed without appropriate clearance.

Can I come with a cold or sinus congestion?

Better to reschedule. Congestion makes equalising uncomfortable. Wait until you're fully better, and check with your GP if you're unsure.

Can I train on the same day?

Yes. Plenty of people book the chamber in the morning for a clear head and train later, or train first and book infrared in the evening to wind down. We'll help you place sessions so you feel ready rather than sluggish.

Can I combine it with the other therapies?

Yes, and most regulars do. A common week is hyperbaric oxygen in the morning for focus and infrared in the evening for wind-down and sleep, with HIFEM or a massage placed where they suit you. Tell us what your week looks like and we'll space them sensibly.

What is the best way to start?

Book a single session, or ring us first on 083 867 2844 if you'd rather talk it through. We'll confirm it suits you, suggest timing — mornings for a clear day, evenings for sleep — and plan your first week so you know exactly what to expect.
