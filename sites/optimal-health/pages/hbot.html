What a session feels like.

You keep your clothes on, sit in the chamber and breathe oxygen through a mask. As the pressure comes up over the first few minutes you'll feel it in your ears, like a plane landing. Swallowing clears it.

After that, most people read, rest or sleep. People often say they feel clear and settled afterwards.

How it works.

  • Session length

    45–60 minutes

    Including the few minutes it takes to come up to pressure and back down.

  • How often

    One or two a week

    Keep this up for three or four weeks, then decide if it's working for you. One session on its own will not do much.

  • Mostly booked for

    Recovery and repair

    People book it between hard training days, after a heavy week, and to sleep better.

At normal pressure, almost all the oxygen you breathe is carried by red blood cells, and they're nearly full already. Under pressure, extra oxygen dissolves into the plasma, the fluid the cells travel in, so your blood carries more of it further into tissue. The body uses oxygen to repair itself.

The effect builds over a course of sessions. Over a block of sessions, the body can also respond by growing new capillaries (the smallest blood vessels) and laying down more collagen.

Why people book it.

Hospitals use hyperbaric oxygen for a short list of medical conditions, at higher pressure and under a doctor. It is also being studied for many more. These are the reasons people come to us. Our chamber is a mild one, for wellness: we don’t diagnose or treat any of these, and on most of them the research is still early.

  • Training recovery

    Between hard training days and before a big event.

    Hyperbaric oxygen is being studied in sport for recovery after hard sessions. Some book it on rest days, because it adds nothing to their training load.

  • Surgery and injury

    After an operation, a strain or a sprain.

    Hospitals use hyperbaric oxygen to help some wounds heal, and it is being studied for recovery after surgery and soft-tissue injury. If you’ve had surgery in the last few months, check with your surgeon or GP before booking.

  • Long COVID

    Long COVID and post-viral fatigue.

    Hyperbaric oxygen is being studied for long COVID, mostly in hospital chambers at higher pressure than ours. People with lingering fatigue after an illness book a block of sessions to see how they feel. Talk to your GP alongside it.

  • Concussion

    After a concussion or a knock to the head.

    Hyperbaric oxygen is being studied after concussion, again mostly at higher pressure. If you’re still recovering from a head injury, get your doctor’s advice first and tell us before you book.

  • Fatigue and burnout

    A heavy few months, poor sleep, running on empty.

    An hour of rest in the chamber, once or twice a week. People often say they sleep better and feel clearer afterwards.

  • Skin and collagen

    Skin that looks tired.

    Over a block of sessions the body can lay down more collagen, which is why hyperbaric oxygen is being studied for skin. Some people pair it with our infrared skin sessions.

Our chamber.

Not all hyperbaric oxygen is the same. The type of chamber, the pressure it runs at and who looks after you all change what a session is.

  • Chamber

    Soft-shell

    A sealed fabric chamber you sit up in, in your own clothes, with room for a book.

  • Pressure

    1.3 ATA

    1.3 times normal air pressure, about what you’d feel three metres under water. Hospital chambers run higher, for medical treatment.

  • Oxygen

    Through a mask

    A concentrator beside the chamber feeds oxygen to a soft mask, so you breathe more of it than the room has, under pressure.

Common questions.

Is this a medical treatment?

No. Hospitals use higher-pressure hyperbaric oxygen for specific medical conditions, under a doctor's care. We offer mild hyperbaric oxygen for general wellness: energy, clarity, sleep and recovery. We don't diagnose or treat medical conditions.

Who is it suitable for?

Anyone who wants more energy, focus, better sleep or recovery. It suits busy professionals, parents, people who train and people who work at a desk. If you have a medical concern, speak to your GP first.

What if I feel claustrophobic?

We can arrange a trial sit-in with no pressure, so you can see the space and practise clearing your ears first. You can talk to us at any point once you're inside, and we can pause or depressurise.

What should I wear, and what can I bring in?

Wear comfortable everyday clothes, and arrive well hydrated. The air in the chamber is oxygen-rich, so don't put on oily lotions, hairspray or perfume just before your session.

You can bring a book. Electronics are not allowed in the chamber. That means no phone, headphones, tablet or e-reader.

For a first session, we'd suggest bringing nothing and just resting.

Will I notice anything after the first session?

It varies. Some people feel clearer after the first session. Others notice changes in sleep or energy after a few sessions. We check in with you on energy and sleep so you can judge whether it's working.

What should I do afterwards?

Drink water and carry on with your day. There's no downtime.

Is it safe, and are there side effects?

Mild hyperbaric oxygen is generally considered safe. Possible effects include temporary ear or sinus pressure and, occasionally, short-lived changes in vision. We supervise every session and stop or adjust if anything feels off.

Who should check with a GP before booking?

If you're pregnant or trying to conceive, have ear or sinus problems, difficulty equalising pressure, heart or respiratory conditions, implanted medical devices, recent surgery, or significant claustrophobia, please talk to your GP before using hyperbaric oxygen.

Are there people who should avoid it altogether?

If you've had a recent pneumothorax, certain ear surgeries, or have devices or conditions that aren't compatible with pressure changes, hyperbaric oxygen may not be suitable for you. Tell us your health history, and we may ask for GP clearance before going ahead.

Can I come while pregnant?

Please get advice from your GP. We don't proceed without appropriate clearance.

Can I come with a cold or sinus congestion?

Please reschedule. Congestion makes it uncomfortable to clear your ears. Wait until you're fully better, and check with your GP if you're unsure.

Can I train on the same day?

Yes. Many people book the chamber in the morning and train later, or train first and book infrared in the evening to wind down.

Can I combine it with the other therapies?

Yes. Most of our regular clients do. For example, some book hyperbaric oxygen in the morning, infrared in the evening, and HIFEM or a massage when they have time. Tell us what your week looks like and we'll help you space them out.
